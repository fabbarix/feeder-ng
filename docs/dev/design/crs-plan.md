# CRS: Courses (plan, migration and teams)

Design steps 1 and 2 for the Courses feature. Decisions: [ADR 0008](../../adr/0008-courses.md) (points 7 to 9 added in step 2) and [ADR 0009](../../adr/0009-soft-cool-down-and-variety.md). Spec: functional-spec §3, §3.1, §4, §7.1 to §7.5, §8, §9, §11, §12, §13, §16 and decisions 29 and 30. Glossary: **Course**, **main Course**, **Whole meal**, **optional Course**, **Course Attendance**, **cooking timeline**, and the updated **Meal Slot**, **Planned Meal**, **Recipe**, **Ready Meal**, **Leftover**, **Slot Rule**, **Cool-down**, **Variety Rule**, **Attendance**, **Favourite**, **Dislike**, **Exclusion**, **Cook Mode**, **Couldn't plan**, **Archived**, **Recipe File**.

The short version: a Planned Meal is keyed by `(date, Meal Slot, Course)` instead of `(date, Meal Slot)`. Everything keyed by a Planned Meal gains the Course. A Meal Slot's Course may be optional, and Members may opt out of a Course (Course Attendance). Cool-down and Variety Rules give way when too few Recipes fit (a relaxed pick), for every Household. Cook Mode works per slot on one cooking timeline. An upgraded Household gets one required Course per slot (the main Course), nobody opting out, and plans as before except that cells that were Couldn't plan for Cool-down or Variety Rule are now filled.

**What changed in step 2** (the user's answers of 2026-11-11), and where: §1.1 (1400 gains `relaxed`, `optional` and the opt-out tables), §1.5 (1410 rewrites Advance Step keys only), §2.1 (scopes of A, B, C, D, F, G; new team K; task CD withdrawn), §2.2 (waves), §2.3 (tests), §2.4 (what A0 and D1 must change now), §3 (questions answered).

**Follow-ups RX and PT** (2026-11-11): Review Exclusions explains the Exclusions (B-RX, C-RX, G2, migration 1430) and preferences on a Recipe as a table (PT, starts now); §2.2 waves and §2.5, design in [rx-pt.md](rx-pt.md).

**Design review fixes** (2026-11-11): the cooking timeline keeps each Course's `schedule.Compute` schedule whole, so one Course is exactly `Compute` (spec §11; K's tests in §2.3); the keep check runs before the walk on every kept fresh Planned Meal, with its own cook left out (spec §7.2; B's tests); the later of two Slot Rule cells that reserve one Recipe gets a relaxed pick (spec §7.2; B's tests); `best_available` is removed (B, with A's store test data); the opt-out tables hang off `meal_slots`, `courses` and `members` only, and a removed Course's opt-outs are deleted explicitly (§1.1; A's tests); D1's prop is `renderRowExtras` with a layout rule (§2.4); open questions 1 and 2 are decided (§3).

## 1. Migration plan

Two migrations, both in the same release: **1400** (team A, §1.1 to §1.4) changes the schema and backfills the Course; **1410** (team G1, §1.5) rewrites the stored reminder keys and merges together with the notification code that writes the new keys. They are split because `push_sent` keys and the code that computes them must change in the same merge: if 1400 rewrote the keys while `internal/notify` still wrote two-part ones, every reminder already sent would be sent again.

### 1.1 Migration 1400: `internal/db/migrations/1400_courses.sql`

It rebuilds tables whose keys change, so its first line is `-- feeder:foreign-keys=off` (the runner then checks `foreign_key_check` before commit, as for 1100). It follows the 12-step rebuild of 1100: create `*_new` with the current definition plus the new columns, copy, drop, rename, recreate indexes. Row ids are kept everywhere (clients cache them). `MAIN` below is `(SELECT id FROM courses WHERE is_main = 1)`, not a literal id.

No column is dropped. Nothing else is keyed by a Planned Meal (checked: `0100`, `0500`, `0600`, `0800`, `0900`, `1000`, `1100`, `1230`). Attendance stays in its tables (`meal_slot_attendees`, `attendance_overrides`, `attendance_override_attendees`); Course Attendance is stored only as opt-outs on top of it, so a new Member, a new Course and an upgraded Household need no rows.

**Course Attendance in the store (team A).** The three opt-out tables hang off `meal_slots`, `courses` and `members` only (each `ON DELETE CASCADE`), **never off `meal_slot_courses`**. The household store saves a slot's child rows by deleting and reinserting them (`internal/household/store/store.go:285` for the usual Attendance in `UpdateSlot`, `batch.go:36` in the multi-day batch), and A saves `meal_slot_courses` the same way; with a foreign key to `meal_slot_courses`, every reorder or batch edit would cascade and wipe the opt-outs of Courses the slot keeps. Without it, deleting and reinserting `meal_slot_courses` is harmless, so a reorder needs no special handling. The price is one explicit step: in the same transaction that saves a slot's Courses (single edit, batch, and Course Delete or Archive), the store deletes the opt-outs, usual and per date (`course_attendance_overrides` rows, which cascade to their `course_attendance_override_opt_outs`), of every Course that is **no longer** on that slot. Deleting a Meal Slot, a Course (removed for good) or a Member still cascades through the foreign keys. A save can therefore never leave behind an opt-out whose Course is not on its slot; and `household.CourseAttendance` is only called for the slot's own Courses, so even such a row would change nothing. The effective Course Attendance is one pure function, `household.CourseAttendance(slot []Attendee, usual []MemberID, date *[]MemberID) []Attendee` (new file `internal/household/courseattendance.go`, team A): the slot's Attendance on that date (usual or date override) less the date's opt-outs for the Course when that date has an override row, else less the usual ones. The plan store, the Date Attendance handler and the plan, Today and board payloads all call it; nothing else subtracts opt-outs.

**Keeping the AUTOINCREMENT counter.** Copying rows into `planned_meals_new` sets its `sqlite_sequence` row to the largest copied id, and `DROP TABLE planned_meals` deletes the old row, so the counter would fall back to `max(id)`: a deleted Planned Meal's id (the newest ones are deleted often, by every replan) would be handed out again, which is what 1100 exists to prevent (clients cache ids). So 1400 carries the old counter over **before** the DROP, and the RENAME then carries it to `planned_meals`:

```sql
DELETE FROM sqlite_sequence WHERE name = 'planned_meals_new';
INSERT INTO sqlite_sequence (name, seq)
  SELECT 'planned_meals_new', seq FROM sqlite_sequence WHERE name = 'planned_meals';
DROP TABLE planned_meals;
ALTER TABLE planned_meals_new RENAME TO planned_meals;
```

The old counter is always at least `max(id)`, so it is the right value; on a fresh database it has no row and none is written. The same applies to any other AUTOINCREMENT table a migration rebuilds; in 1400 that is only `planned_meals` (`leftovers` and `slot_rules` have a plain `INTEGER PRIMARY KEY`, and `plan_unfilled` a composite key).

### 1.2 Idempotency

- The runner records `1400_courses.sql` in `schema_migrations` and does not apply it twice; the file itself uses plain `CREATE`/`ALTER`, as every other migration.
- Every backfill reads `MAIN` by the flag, so the file gives the same result on a fresh install (no rows to backfill) and on an upgrade.
- The startup replan (reason Startup, spec §7.4) follows it, so Couldn't plan reasons are rewritten under the new keys straight away.

### 1.3 Snapshot and going back

- Migrations cannot be undone. Before applying 1400 on a database that already has `planned_meals` rows, Feeder takes a verified snapshot into `<data>/backups` as `feeder-pre-1400.db`. A failure to take it stops start-up with a clear log line; a fresh install takes none.
- The existing `backup.Snapshot(ctx, db, dir, day)` cannot write it: it only writes `feeder-<YYYY-MM-DD>.db` and refuses any `day` that is not a date. Team A adds, all owned by A:
  - `backup.SnapshotNamed(ctx, db, dir, name string) (Info, error)` in `internal/backup/backup.go`: the same VACUUM INTO, `integrity_check`, 0600 file, temporary file and rename, and folder lock as `Snapshot` (one shared private helper), for a `name` matching `^feeder-pre-\d{4}\.db$` only (anything else is an error, so it can never overwrite a daily snapshot). An existing file of that name is replaced: the snapshot is only taken while 1400 is pending, so the database is pre-1400 by definition and the newer copy is the right one (say after going back, running the old image for a week, then upgrading again).
  - `db.Pending(ctx, d, name string) (bool, error)` in `internal/db/db.go`: true when `schema_migrations` does not record `name` yet (a missing `schema_migrations` table, on a brand-new file, counts as pending); and `db.NeedsPre1400Snapshot(ctx, d) (bool, error)`: 1400 is pending **and** a `planned_meals` table exists with at least one row.
  - In `cmd/feeder/main.go`, between `db.Open` and `db.MigrateEmbedded`: if `db.NeedsPre1400Snapshot`, call `backup.SnapshotNamed(ctx, d, filepath.Join(cfg.DataDir, backup.DirName), "feeder-pre-1400.db")`, and stop start-up on an error.
- The file sits in `<data>/backups` next to the daily snapshots, but it is **not listed in Settings** and never pruned: `backup.List` and `nameRE` (`^feeder-(\d{4}-\d{2}-\d{2})\.db$`) ignore it, and `Prune` only removes what `List` returns. It stays until someone deletes it by hand; the upgrade note says where it is.
- Going back: stop Feeder, restore `<data>/backups/feeder-pre-1400.db` as `feeder.db`, and start the image you ran before. The CHANGELOG upgrade note says so (team H).
- If 1400 fails, its transaction rolls back and start-up stops naming the file (runner behaviour); the database is unchanged.

### 1.4 Migration tests (`internal/db/courses_migration_test.go`, team A)

Seed a database migrated `upTo(t, "1400_")` with: two Meal Slots, three Recipes (one Ready Meal, one archived), a Pinned, a Cooked and a Not cooked Planned Meal, a Planned Meal served from a Leftover (both a planned source and an actual Leftover), a Couldn't plan reason, a Slot Rule by Recipe and one by Tag, a Planned Meal scoped and a Recipe scoped cook timer, and an Advance Step mark. Then delete the Planned Meal with the highest id (so `sqlite_sequence` for `planned_meals` is above `max(id)`), note the counter, `MigrateEmbedded` and assert:

1. four Courses in order, exactly one main (Main), none archived;
2. every Recipe and Meal Slot has exactly the main Course; the Ready Meal is Whole meal, the others are not;
3. every Planned Meal, Leftover, reason and Slot Rule kept its id and fields and has `course_id = MAIN`; `from_course_id` is set exactly where `from_date` is;
4. `UNIQUE (date, meal_slot_id, course_id)` holds and a second Course on the same slot and date can be inserted;
5. Planned Meal scopes gained `/MAIN`, the Recipe scope did not; `push_sent` is unchanged (1410's job);
6. `PRAGMA foreign_key_check` is empty and every index exists (compare `sqlite_master`);
7. a fresh database migrates to the same schema (`sqlite_master` text) as the upgraded one;
8. the second `MigrateEmbedded` is a no-op;
9. `sqlite_sequence` for `planned_meals` equals the counter noted before (not `max(id)`), there is no `planned_meals_new` row left, and the next inserted Planned Meal gets a new id above the removed one; on a fresh database `planned_meals` has no `sqlite_sequence` row until its first insert;
10. every `meal_slot_courses` row has `optional = 0`, every Planned Meal `relaxed = 0`, and the three opt-out tables are empty; deleting a `meal_slot_courses` row does **not** touch any opt-out (no foreign key to it); deleting a Meal Slot, a Course or a Member cascades to their opt-outs and date overrides (and the overrides to their opt-outs).

The snapshot gets its own tests: `SnapshotNamed` writes a file that passes `integrity_check`, refuses `feeder-2026-11-11.db` and any other name outside the pattern, replaces an existing file of its name, and the file is not in `List` nor removed by `Prune`; `NeedsPre1400Snapshot` is false on a fresh database, false once 1400 is recorded, false with an empty `planned_meals`, and true before 1400 with rows; `cmd/feeder` takes the snapshot before migrating (a test on the small start-up function it calls, not on `main`).

### 1.5 Migration 1410: `internal/db/migrations/1410_reminder_keys.sql` (team G1)

It rewrites `push_sent.key` to the per-Course format of spec §12, and it merges **together with G1's `internal/notify` change** that writes those keys, never before or after it.

- Advance Step identity `<date>/<slot>/<stepId>` becomes `<date>/<slot>/<course>/<stepId>`. Start cooking and Confirm Cooked stay `<date>/<slot>`: both are one reminder per slot (Start cooking fires at the start of the slot's cooking timeline, spec §12), so their keys are **not** touched. (Step 1 of this design rewrote Start cooking keys too; step 2 drops that.)
- Keys are `<type>:<identity>@<RFC 3339>` (`internal/notify/compute.go`, `candidateKey`), and the date is 10 characters, so for `advanceStep:` insert `'/' || MAIN` before the `/` that follows the slot id (the second `/` after the type prefix; the first `/` after the date sits before the slot id). The prefix `advanceStep:` is 12 characters and the date 10, so the date ends at 22, the `/` before the slot id is 23 and the slot id starts at 24. With `p = instr(substr(key, 24), '/')`, the new key is `substr(key, 1, 22 + p) || '/' || MAIN || substr(key, 23 + p)`. Example, with `MAIN` = 5: `advanceStep:2026-11-12/3/17@2026-11-12T16:30:00Z` becomes `advanceStep:2026-11-12/3/5/17@2026-11-12T16:30:00Z`. `MAIN` is read by the flag, as in 1400. Plain SQL, no foreign-key pragma.
- Test (`internal/db/reminder_keys_migration_test.go`, G1): seed `push_sent` rows of each type at `upTo(t, "1410_")`, migrate, assert the Advance Step rewrite, Start cooking, Confirm Cooked and the other types unchanged, and a second `MigrateEmbedded` a no-op. A notify test asserts that a reminder sent before the migration is not sent again after it.
- G1 owns these two new files; they touch nothing of A's, and the Project Manager grants them in `internal/db/**`.

Decided at the design review (the Project Manager accepted the defaults of the former open questions on these two points):

1. **Changing Course Attendance from the Plan.** Decided: not now (the Project Manager accepted the default). The Planned Meal sheet shows who eats a Course but cannot change it; per-date opt-outs are set in the Date Attendance sheet (Settings > Meal Slots), as per-date Attendance is today. A "Change for this date" shortcut from the sheet can follow once the sheet is a shared component.

The brief listed "attendance-based dislikes" among the hard rules, but a Dislike has only ever been a score term: Dislikes lower the score (counted over the Course Attendance) and are never a filter, so they block neither a strict pick nor a relaxed one. Exclusions are the hard per-Member rule.
